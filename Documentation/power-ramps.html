Power ramps

Summary

A power ramp means the laser power changes along a single hatch or polyline vector. The 3MF toolpath extension models this with profile modifiers and per-segment modification factors. The EOS toolpath interface has no ramp primitive: laser power is a modal state that applies to every following vector. Lib3MFToolpath4EOS therefore discretizes a ramp into sub-vectors of constant power, each at the mean power of its interval, so the deposited energy is preserved. Scan speed and focus are treated the same way.

By default, a ramped hatch is written as one continuous polyline with the new values before every sub-vector, and hatches that touch are joined into one polyline. Power changes survive both directions: 3MF toolpaths to EOS tasks, and EOS tasks to 3MF toolpaths.

The 3MF modifier system

This is how the toolpath API of the vendored lib3mf models overrides. A profile parameter is a default. A modifier says how per-segment factors override it.

ElementMeaning
Profile parameterA double value, for example laserpower = 280. It applies wherever a segment carries no factors.
ModifierAttached to one double parameter (ToolpathProfile::AddModifier). It names a factor channel (F, G, H or E), a type (constant, linear or nonlinear) and a range: MinValue at factor 0 and MaxValue at factor 1.
Segment factorsNormalized values per hatch or per point, stored in the layer data for one channel.

The effective value at position t ∈ [0, 1] along a hatch is

P(t) = MinValue + clamp(f(t), 0, 1) · (MaxValue - MinValue)

Lib3MFToolpath4EOS does not clamp silently: in strict mode (the default) a factor outside [0, 1] fails the conversion with MODIFICATIONFACTOROUTOFRANGE. With strict mode off it is clamped, counted in factorsClamped and logged as FACTOR_CLAMPED.

What EOS can express

In the toolpath write interface, WriteSetLaserPower(power, delay), WriteSetScanSpeed and WriteSetFocus set their value for all vectors that follow, and WriteHatch(start, end) and WritePolyCoord carry no values. A change between PolyCoord records applies to the next vector. A ramp is therefore written in one of these forms:

ModeRecordsEffect
Polyline (default)SetLaserPower(p0) PolyStart(A) SetLaserPower(p1) PolyCoord(X1) … PolyEnd(B) The laser stays on, so there are no on/off artefacts at the joints.
SplitHatchesSetLaserPower(pk) Hatch(Xk-1, Xk) per sub-vector Each sub-vector switches the laser off and on and may skywrite, which can cause over- or under-exposure at the joints. In this mode, hatches are never joined into polylines.
Flattenone hatch at the length-weighted mean valuesThe ramp is lost, but the energy is the same.
RejectnoneThe conversion fails with POWERRAMPREJECTED and the position of the ramp.

Hatches are never joined with StartStripeSegment or EndLine, because these records do not form one continuous line. Every SetLaserPower carries the PowerDelay of the settings (190 µs by default), also between the points of a polyline.

Settings

The power ramp settings are part of the conversion settings, which are passed to CreateTaskFrom3MFToolpath and CreateRawTaskWriter.

auto pSettings = pContext->CreateToolpathConversionSettings();
pSettings->SetMaxPowerStep(1.0);
pSettings->SetMinSubVectorLength(0.05);
pSettings->SetPowerRampMode(Lib3MF4EOS::eEOSPowerRampMode::Polyline);
SettingDefaultMeaning
MaxPowerStep1 WLargest power difference between neighbouring sub-vectors.
MaxSpeedStep10 mm/sLargest scan speed difference between neighbouring sub-vectors.
MaxFocusStep0.05Largest difference of the EOS focus value (0 to 1) between neighbouring sub-vectors.
MinSubVectorLength0.05 mmShortest sub-vector. On a short vector it limits the number of sub-vectors and wins over the step limits.
PowerRampModePolylinePolyline, SplitHatches, Flatten or Reject, see above.
PowerDelay190 µsDelay passed with every SetLaserPower.

The values must be positive and finite, otherwise the setter fails with INVALIDCONVERSIONSETTINGS. The conversion log counts ramped hatches and sub-vectors, and warns about every flattened ramp. For a linear piece over length L whose power goes from p1 to p2, speed from s1 to s2 and focus from z1 to z2, the number of sub-vectors and their values are

m  = max(ceil(|p2 - p1| / MaxPowerStep), ceil(|s2 - s1| / MaxSpeedStep), ceil(|z2 - z1| / MaxFocusStep))
n  = max(1, min(m, floor(L / MinSubVectorLength), 100000))
pk = p1 + (k + 0.5) / n * (p2 - p1)        // mean power of sub-vector k, k = 0 … n-1; speed and focus alike

Values that differ by less than 10-6 count as equal, and a record is only written when its value changes.

3MF to EOS

CreateTaskFrom3MFToolpath reads the laserpower, laserspeed and eos:focus modifiers of each segment's profile, with all three modifier types. Segments without factors use the profile's values. Each modifier can use its own factor channel.

SegmentConversion
Hatch, constantBoth points of a hatch get the value of its factor. If the two factors of a hatch differ, the conversion fails with UNEQUALCONSTANTHATCH; with RefuseUnequalConstantHatch off, the factor of the second point is used and CONSTANT_HATCH_UNEQUAL logged.
Hatch, linearThe points get the values of f1 and f2; the writer discretizes the ramp.
Hatch, nonlinearThe hatch is split at the interpolation points of all its modifiers into collinear sub-hatches, each with a linear ramp. The writer treats them as one line again. Parameters that do not strictly increase in (0, 1) are an error.
Polyline or loopEvery point gets its values. A vector whose values change is discretized right away, following PowerRampMode; a loop is closed with its first point. Zero-length vectors are skipped. With a constant modifier the value is a step: each vector is written at the value of the point it ends at, without sub-vectors.

Pulse settings

EOS can only change the pulse settings between blocks, so eos:pulsedwaveperiodtime and eos:pulsedwavepowerontime (SetLaserPulse) accept constant modifiers only. In a hatch segment, every hatch can have its own value; the segment is split into blocks wherever a value changes. A polyline or loop is one continuous mark, so its value must be the same on every vector (the factor of the initial jump is ignored). Otherwise the conversion fails, because splitting the polyline would switch the laser off in between.

Delays and dwell times

ParameterEffect
eos:predelay (µs)A delay before the segment.
eos:postdelay (µs)A delay after every hatch, or after the end of a polyline or loop.
postwaittime, with or without modifier (any type and factor channel)A dwell after every vector, in the DwellTimeUnit of the settings: after each hatch (the factor at its end; for a constant modifier both ends must be equal), and after each polyline vector (the factor of its end point). A dwell inside a polyline ends the polyline there and starts a new one at the same point. If eos:postdelay is also given, both are added.

Toolpaths with dwell times fail with DWELLTIMEUNITMISSING (log code DWELL_UNIT_MISSING) while DwellTimeUnit is Unset. Hatch segments are split into blocks where the dwell changes.

Other parameters

The conversion fails with INVALIDPARAM if a segment carries factors for a modifier on a parameter that EOS cannot change between vectors, such as laserindex, eos:exposeddepth or parameters of other namespaces. A modifier whose factor channel carries no data in a segment has no effect there and is not checked.

Raw layers

A raw layer stores power, speed and focus per point in sEOSTaskPoint, so it holds ramps without loss. A LaserPower or ScanSpeed of 0 or less uses the block's value. A focus of 0 (fully defocused) is valid, so LaserFocus is only used if HasLaserFocus is true; without a focus, the scanner stays fully focused (1.0). A point that is all zero uses the block values.

BlockMeaning of the point values
Hatch blockThe values at the point. A hatch ramps linearly from its first to its second point.
Polyline blockThe values of the vector that ends at this point. The first point's values are ignored.

A block's PostDelay is written after every hatch (touching collinear hatches count as one), or after the end of a polyline. BeamShapeProfile sets the beam shape of its vectors with EOS SDK 2.26 and later; -1 keeps the current one. With older SDKs, a profile above 0 fails the conversion in strict mode; otherwise it is left out and SDK_FEATURE_UNAVAILABLE is logged once.

When an EOS task is read, every point gets the power, speed and focus in force, and the block values are those of the block's first point. A speed or focus change inside a polyline does not end the block. The delay of a SetLaserPower record is not read as a pre-delay, because the writer adds the PowerDelay of its settings to every power change.

Writing the task

The raw task writer goes through each hatch block and collects chains of hatches that touch within 10-6 mm and continue in the same direction, for example the pieces of a nonlinear ramp. A chain with a ramp is discretized in the configured mode. Polyline blocks are written as polylines, with the changed values before every coordinate.

Joining

In the default JoinTouching mode, a run of hatches in which each starts within JoinTolerance of the previous end, in any direction, is written as one polyline. The values are written between the points, and a gap within the tolerance becomes a short vector with the values of the next hatch. A hatch that touches nothing is written with WriteHatch, unless it has a ramp. In AllPolylines mode, every hatch is written as a polyline, and touching hatches are joined the same way. In SplitHatches ramp mode, hatches are never joined.

Runs continue across hatch blocks, and are broken by a dwell (PostDelay), a pre-delay, or a change of part, scanner, exposure type, skywriting, exposure step, laser pulse, depth or beam shape. Inside a run, a power change smaller than HatchPowerDeadband, compared with the power in force, is not written.

With JoinContours on (the default), a polyline block that starts within JoinTolerance of the end of the polyline block before it is appended to it, under the same conditions. A closed loop is never extended.

Layer state

Every setting is written to the selected scanner when it changes, and again for each scanner in every layer. Before the first vector of a scanner in a layer, LayerOpening writes SetBeamShape(standard, 0) and a delay of LayerOpeningDelay. This needs EOS SDK 2.26; with older SDKs it is left out and SDK_FEATURE_UNAVAILABLE is logged once.

EOS to 3MF

When EOS tasks or computed toolpaths are written as 3MF, each block whose power varies gets a profile with a laserpower modifier on channel F, from 0 W to a maximum rounded up to the next 100 W (at least 100 W). Blocks of similar power share a profile.

BlockWritten as
Hatches with constant power per hatchConstant modifier, one factor per hatch.
Hatches with rampsLinear modifier, factors at both points.
Polyline with power changesLinear modifier, factors per point. A power step at a joint is written as a repeated point, so the step stays a step and does not become a ramp.

lib3mf 2.4.0 writes one factor list per segment, and power uses it. Speed and focus changes are therefore kept by cutting the block: a polyline is cut at every point where speed or focus change, and hatches are grouped into runs of equal speed and focus. Each part gets a profile with its own laserspeed and eos:focus (left out when fully focused). The parts of a polyline share their joint points, so with JoinContours on (the default) they are written as one polyline again when the 3MF is converted back to EOS. A speed or focus ramp inside a single hatch is stored as its mean. A delay in front of a block (eos:predelay) stays with the first part only, and each part carries the EOS timestamp of its own first point.

Power, speed and focus are no longer rounded to whole numbers.
